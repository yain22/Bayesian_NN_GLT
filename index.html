What does “width” mean?

Layer width counts neurons: four in layer 1 and eight in layer 2. Bell width describes the spread of possible weight values before observing data. In the paper, the prior standard deviation for one unit’s weights is σλ, where σ is the observation-error standard deviation and λ is the unit scale. These examples use σ = 1.

Can a unit escape strong shrinkage?

Some units can have a much larger scale than the rest. The GLT tail shape, ξ, controls how common these unusually large scales are within a layer.

What changes when ξ changes?

Each weight still has a Gaussian bell conditional on its unit scale. The heavier tail belongs to the distribution of unit scales. This comparison holds the layer scale τ fixed. In the full GLT model the prior on τ also depends on ξ.

When does a neuron disappear?

Our GL model and the horseshoe networks of Ghosh, Yao and Doshi-Velez both group incoming weights by neuron. Choose how to use a small fitted scale.

Example fitted probability that the unit scale is below 0.001. Their rule prunes when this probability exceeds 90%.

How do the two studies use shrinkage?

Ghosh and colleagues fit grouped horseshoe and regularized-horseshoe networks, then study predictive accuracy and network size after pruning. We retain every hidden unit and compare prediction across G, GL and GLT at each architecture. A small incoming scale alone cannot establish that a ReLU unit is redundant.

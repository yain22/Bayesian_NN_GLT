From unit 2 in layer 1 to the output

The pruning example removes this unit. The equations below describe its connections before removal.

The three predictors feed unit 2 in layer 1. The edge from the square input 1 carries b21, the last entry of 𝐰21. ReLU takes the positive part of the weighted sum.

h21(𝐱)=max{0,b21+∑j=13w21,jxj}.

Each of the eight units in layer 2 receives h21(𝐱). In unit j, the term from this connection is wj2,2h21(𝐱). The eight second-layer activations then enter the output. Shrinking this one first-layer unit leaves each predictor connected through the other three units.

For G the probability drops to zero at z=1, below the log axis. Under GLT, increasing ξ1 makes large coefficient scales more probable at fixed τ1. In the joint prior, the distribution of τ1 also depends on ξ1.

Fitted scales · Model comparison

Pruning unit 2 in layer 1

Ghosh, Yao and Doshi-Velez give the 4 coefficients entering one hidden unit a common local scale. Our GL-BNN assigns each of those coefficients its own local scale. In their notation τ21 is the unit’s local scale and υ1 is the shared layer scale. Their post-fitting rule removes unit 2 if

Prq(τ21υ1<δ)>p0.

Here q is their fitted variational distribution, δ=10−3, and p0=0.9. In the grouped-horseshoe example, move the hypothetical fitted probability across the threshold.

For our models, Prq(σλ21,1<ϵ) summarizes mass near a small conditional weight SD. All 4 units remain in layer 1 of the fitted architecture. ReLU homogeneity allows an outgoing weight to compensate for a rescaling of the incoming weights and bias.

G-BNN · Step 1 of 12

Set up the regression network

Use the training rows to define the scales of the inputs and response. Carry those same scales forward to prediction.

Let K0=p and let Kl be the width of hidden layer l. Its Ml=Kl(Kl−1+1) coefficients include a bias for every receiving unit. Within a layer write a=(k,j) for one of these coefficients. The output vector v includes its intercept and has length do=KL+1.

h0=x,hl=max{0,Wl(hl−1⊤,1)⊤}
fW(x)=v⊤(hL⊤,1)⊤,Yi∣W,σ2~N(fW(xi),σ2)

Fit on predictors and responses standardized by their training sample means and standard deviations. The paper uses a Jeffreys prior p(σ2)∝1/σ2. The hidden-weight prior conditional on the scales is wa~N(0,σ2λa2). The output prior is v∣u~N(0,uI) with u=κ~C+(0,5).

Variational factors and inverse-gamma convention

Each scalar hidden or output weight has its own N(mj,sj2) factor. The noise variance, output-scale variables and variables in the selected shrinkage hierarchy have separate factors. Independence under this fitted approximation lets their moments multiply in a coordinate update.

IG(a,b):q(z)=baΓ(a)z−a−1exp(−b/z),z>0

For q(X)=IG(a,b), write ϕX=Eq[X−1]=a/b and ζX=Eq[logX]=logb−ψ(a). We abbreviate the noise precision ϕσ2 to ϕ. The same inverse-gamma shape and scale convention is used throughout.

G-BNN · Step 2 of 12

Initialize a single fit

Train one unpenalized network and use its fitted coefficients to initialize the variational means.

Before the warm start, sample every coefficient in a weight matrix, including biases and readout coefficients, independently from N(0,2/d), with d the number of incoming units plus one for the bias in that matrix. The standard warm start takes 600 full-batch Adam steps on squared-error loss at a fixed rate of 0.003. Set each variational standard deviation to 0.01, within the supplied log-standard-deviation bounds. The variational optimizer starts with fresh first and second moment accumulators. The lr control applies to this variational optimizer.

q(σ2)=IG(3,1),q(u)=IG(2,2),q(ρκ)=IG(1,1.04)

The starting value of ϕ is therefore 3. The output-scale auxiliary ρκ comes from u∣ρκ~IG(1/2,1/ρκ) and ρκ~IG(1/2,5−2). The next step initializes the hidden scales for the selected prior.

Each start in Figure S10 generates a complete fit. A given setting and start use the same data and starting weights across the three priors. Ten such starts for each of nine settings and three priors give 270 trajectories. Each trajectory has its own 60 outer cycles.

G-BNN · Step 3 of 12

Initialize the G shrinkage hierarchy

Introduce the auxiliary factors that give closed-form scale updates.

Repeat the following initialization in every layer l. Define Tl=τl2. The half-Cauchy layer scale has prior scale 1/Ml, where Ml counts every incoming weight and bias.

τl~C+(0,1/Ml),Tl∣χτ,l~IG(1/2,1/χτ,l),χτ,l~IG(1/2,Ml2)

Initialize q(Tl)=IG(2,2) and q(χτ,l)=IG(1,1).

Every coefficient in this layer has λa=τl and Pa=ϕTl. Initially Pa=1 and ϕPa=3.

G-BNN · Step 4 of 12

Set the current shrinkage precisions

Record the full-batch objective at initialization. At the beginning of each cycle, read the latest variance and scale moments.

Pa=Eq[λa−2],πa=ϕPa,πo,j=Eq[u−1]

The selected hierarchy supplies Pa for each hidden weight or bias. The noise precision multiplies hidden-weight precisions in Gaussian regression. All output coefficients have precision πo,j, independent of the observation variance. Keep these precisions fixed during this cycle’s weight updates.

For the baseline full-batch ELBO at cycle 0, generate 32 independent standard-normal perturbation vectors and pair each with its negative. Save this monitoring bank. The weight optimizer and the variance update use separate draws. The objective-monitoring step below shows the calculation at cycle 0 and every subsequent checkpoint.

G-BNN · Step 5 of 12

Backpropagate through sampled networks

Each proposed update changes the variational means and log standard deviations of all hidden and output coefficients together.

Write ℓj=logsj. For one attempt, generate two independent standard-normal vectors and their negatives, giving four perturbations ϵ(b). Draw a complete set of weights W(b)=m+s⊙ϵ(b) for each forward pass. The sampled weight objective, up to terms fixed in this block, is

J^=−ϕ2B∑b=1B∑i=1n{yi−fW(b)(xi)}2−12∑jπj(mj2+sj2)+∑jℓj.

At draw b, begin at the output with the residual score ϕ(yi−fW(b)(xi)). Backpropagate this score through the sampled weights and the active ReLU gates. Denote the resulting derivative of the sampled data log likelihood with respect to weight wj(b) by gj(b).

∂J^∂mj=1B∑bgj(b)−πjmj,∂J^∂ℓj=1B∑bgj(b)sjϵj(b)+1−πjsj2.

The factors 1 and sjϵj(b) are the derivatives of wj(b) with respect to mj and ℓj. The terms outside the averages are the exact Gaussian prior and entropy gradients. ReLU activations and their composition across layers generally prevent a closed-form Gaussian weight update.

Trace one hidden connection

For a unit with preactivation z and activation h=max(0,z), multiply the derivative arriving from the next layer by 1{z>0}. Multiply that result by the sending activation to obtain the derivative for its incoming weight. Use a sending activation of one for a bias. Sum over training rows. The implementation uses derivative zero at z=0.

As an illustration, let f=vmax(0,wx) on one row with x=2, y=3, w=0.5, v=1 and ϕ=1. The forward pass gives f=1. The output score is 3−1=2, the gate is open, and the derivative for w is 2×1×1×2=4. If s=0.1 and ϵ=1, this draw contributes 4 to the likelihood gradient for the mean and 0.4 to that for the log standard deviation.

G-BNN · Step 6 of 12

Take ten successive weight-update attempts

Adam converts the weight gradients into a proposed change. Each attempt starts from the accepted or retained state of the preceding attempt.

Divide the gradient of J^ by n, clip its Euclidean norm at 10, and form the bias-corrected Adam direction. The defaults use moments with decay 0.9 and 0.999, a denominator stabilizer of 10−8 and an initial step size of 0.003. If the direction has nonpositive inner product with the gradient, use the gradient divided by the Adam denominator.

θtrial=θ+0.0032−bd,b=0,…,10,θ=(m,logs)

Clip trial log standard deviations to [−15,3]. Score the current and trial parameters with the same four perturbations and fixed prior moments. Accept the first finite candidate whose sampled objective meets or exceeds the current value. If all 11 step sizes fail, keep the current weight factors. Adam’s gradient moments and the attempt counter advance on every attempt, including rejections, and carry over between cycles. Draw fresh perturbations for the next attempt.

After ten attempts, refresh the weight moments and update the variance and shrinkage factors. Ten controls how often those factors are refreshed. A single attempt per cycle is also possible. The study used ten for all three priors.

G-BNN · Step 7 of 12

Update the observation variance

Use the new weight moments, the current hidden-scale precisions, and a fresh estimate of the residual sum of squares.

Ha=ma2+sa2,DG=∑lMl,R≈116∑b=116∑i(yi−fW(b)(xi))2

Use eight fresh antithetic pairs for R. With the Jeffreys prior on σ2 and fitted layer multipliers equal to one, the update is

q(σ2)=IG(n+DG2,R+∑l∑a∈IlPaHa2).

The conditional normal priors on hidden coefficients supply the DG contribution to the shape and the shrinkage-weighted sum in the scale. The output coefficients have their own prior scale. Use the new ϕ=Eq[1/σ2] in the hidden-hierarchy updates that follow.

Proper noise prior and classification

With a user-supplied IG(a0,b0) noise prior, add a0 and b0 to the displayed shape and scale. For binary or unordered categorical outcomes, omit the observation-variance factor, use hidden prior precision Pa and backpropagate the logistic or reference-class softmax log likelihood. The downloadable R examples on the computation page use Gaussian regression.

G-BNN · Step 8 of 12

Update the output scale and its auxiliary

Pool the expected squared output weights, including the intercept.

Ho=∑j=1do(mo,j2+so,j2),q(u)=IG(do+12,Ho2+ϕρκ)
q(ρκ)=IG(1,ϕu+5−2)

Update q(u) first and use its new inverse moment ϕu in the auxiliary update. The next weight block uses ϕu for all readout weights. Each hidden layer now updates the scale factors specific to the selected prior.

G-BNN · Step 9 of 12

Update the layer scale and its auxiliary

Pool the expected squares of every hidden weight and bias in the layer.

q(Tl)=IG(Ml+12,ϕχτ,l+ϕ2∑a∈IlHa)
q(χτ,l)=IG(1,Ml2+ϕTl).

Update the layer auxiliary using the new inverse moment of Tl. The Ml2 term comes from the inverse square of the half-Cauchy prior scale 1/Ml.

For the next cycle every coefficient in this layer receives the same Pa=ϕTl. The ELBO uses Eq[logλa2]=ζTl.

G-BNN · Step 10 of 12

Calculate the full variational objective

After updating all factors, record one ELBO estimate for the entire network. This is the value shown in the replay.

L(q)=Eq[logp(y∣W,σ2)]+Eq[logp(W,z)]+H(q)

Here z includes the observation variance, scale variables and auxiliaries. For the Gaussian likelihood use the fixed monitoring bank from cycle 0. With the current m,s, form the 64 weight draws m+s⊙ϵ(b) and evaluate the expected log likelihood as

L^y=−n2{log(2π)+ζσ2}−ϕ2B∑b=1B∑i=1n{yi−fW(b)(xi)}2,B=64.

Add the expected log densities of the Gaussian weights and their scale hierarchy, the noise log prior and the entropy of every fitted factor. The likelihood is the sole Monte Carlo term. G and GL use their inverse-gamma moments. GLT also uses normalized scalar quadrature. Under the Jeffreys variance prior use Eq[logp(σ2)]=−ζσ2 with the arbitrary additive constant set to zero.

Contributions from a hidden weight and an output weight
Eq[logp(wa∣σ2,λa)]=−12{log(2π)+ζσ2+Eq[logλa2]+ϕPaHa}
Eq[logp(vj∣u)]=−12{log(2π)+ζu+ϕuHo,j}

Here Ho,j=mo,j2+so,j2. Each Gaussian weight or bias adds entropy 12log(2πesj2). Each inverse-gamma factor contributes a+logb+logΓ(a)−(1+a)ψ(a) to the entropy. Include conditional-prior normalizers that depend on a random parent, especially the GLT tail shape. The source link gives the full sum, including the scale priors and auxiliaries.

Monte Carlo uncertainty and the stopping rule

Pair the likelihood contributions for each perturbation and its negative. For two consecutive checkpoints take the within-pair mean of the two changes. The sample standard deviation of the 32 paired changes divided by 32 gives the reported Monte Carlo standard error of the gain. Fixed perturbations produce different realized weights as m and s change.

Backtracking tests a sampled weight objective with fixed scales. The full monitored ELBO can decrease after a cycle, and a small one-cycle change cannot establish that the parameters have stabilized. At the default budget, record cycles 0 through 60. Return the final factors and separately reevaluate the endpoint with 256 fresh antithetic draws. The preset budget gives the three priors the same number of weight-update attempts.

fit$engine$trace[, c("outer", "elbo", "gain", "paired_gain_mcse")]
fit$engine$components
fit$engine$endpoint$value

G-BNN · Step 11 of 12

Choose settings for a new fit

Use the R control list to change the fitting schedule and Monte Carlo accuracy.

The number of cycles is outer (default 60). The number of successive attempts inside each cycle is weight_steps (10). The unpenalized warm start has its own init_steps (600). Each of these takes a positive integer. To compare budgets on a full-batch trajectory, hold the data, architecture, prior, initialization seed and other controls fixed and increase outer.

control = list(outer = 60L, weight_steps = 10L, init_steps = 600L,
               lr = 0.003, grad_clip = 10, max_backtracks = 10L,
               logsd_bounds = c(-15, 3), weight_draws = 4L,
               noise_draws = 16L, monitor_draws = 64L, endpoint_draws = 256L)

weight_draws controls each stochastic gradient. noise_draws controls the residual expectation for the noise update. monitor_draws sets the full-batch ELBO bank. endpoint_draws sets the independent endpoint bank. The four draw counts must be even integers of at least two. The step-size cap lr, gradient-norm cap grad_clip, backtracking limit and bounds on log standard deviations affect the weight optimizer.

Model settings, minibatches and prediction draws

Specify hidden, prior, geometry, outcome, seed and noise_prior directly in gltbnn_fit(). This walkthrough uses coefficient geometry, continuous outcomes and the default Jeffreys prior. For a proper inverse-gamma prior, supply a named vector with positive shape and scale. The code examples for each prior on the computation page fit the same training rows from the trees dataset.

By default batch_size is NULL and each update sees the full training data. If a batch size is supplied, the optimizer scales the likelihood gradient by n/b on a batch of b rows. The noise update uses the rows pooled over that cycle with the corresponding rescaling. Full-data ELBO and MC standard-error fields are then unavailable. Gaussian minibatch fits record pooled-batch RMSE.

The draws argument to predict() controls prediction separately and defaults to 256 independent draws. Increasing it reduces Monte Carlo variation in predictions for an already fitted model.

G-BNN · Step 12 of 12

Return the fit and predict a new response

Average predictions across the final Gaussian weight factors and convert the regression mean back to the response units.

Y^(x*)=y¯+sy{1S∑b=1SfW(b)(x*std)},W(b)~qθ(W)

Apply the training predictor centers and standard deviations to the new input. For a predictive draw of a future response, also draw an observation variance from its fitted factor and add Gaussian observation noise on the standardized scale. In classification, average the draw-specific class probabilities.

library(gltbnn)
x <- datasets::trees[c("Girth", "Height")]
y <- datasets::trees$Volume
test <- seq(5L, nrow(x), by = 5L)
fit <- gltbnn_fit(x[-test, ], y[-test], hidden = c(3L, 2L),
                  prior = "G", seed = 31L,
                  control = list(outer = 60L, weight_steps = 10L))
predict(fit, x[test, ], type = "mean", draws = 256L, seed = 32L)

Introduce the auxiliary factors that give closed-form scale updates.

λa=τlra,Ua∣χr,a~IG(1/2,1/χr,a),χr,a~IG(1/2,1).

Calculate the full variational objective

After updating all factors, record one ELBO estimate for the entire network. This is the value shown in the replay.

L(q)=Eq[logp(y∣W,σ2)]+Eq[logp(W,z)]+H(q)

Here z includes the observation variance, scale variables and auxiliaries. For the Gaussian likelihood use the fixed monitoring bank from cycle 0. With the current m,s, form the 64 weight draws m+s⊙ϵ(b) and evaluate the expected log likelihood as

L^y=−n2{log(2π)+ζσ2}−ϕ2B∑b=1B∑i=1n{yi−fW(b)(xi)}2,B=64.

Add the expected log densities of the Gaussian weights and their scale hierarchy, the noise log prior and the entropy of every fitted factor. The likelihood is the sole Monte Carlo term. G and GL use their inverse-gamma moments. GLT also uses normalized scalar quadrature. Under the Jeffreys variance prior use Eq[logp(σ2)]=−ζσ2 with the arbitrary additive constant set to zero.

Contributions from a hidden weight and an output weight
Eq[logp(wa∣σ2,λa)]=−12{log(2π)+ζσ2+Eq[logλa2]+ϕPaHa}
Eq[logp(vj∣u)]=−12{log(2π)+ζu+ϕuHo,j}

Here Ho,j=mo,j2+so,j2. Each Gaussian weight or bias adds entropy 12log(2πesj2). Each inverse-gamma factor contributes a+logb+logΓ(a)−(1+a)ψ(a) to the entropy. Include conditional-prior normalizers that depend on a random parent, especially the GLT tail shape. The source link gives the full sum, including the scale priors and auxiliaries.

Monte Carlo uncertainty and the stopping rule

Pair the likelihood contributions for each perturbation and its negative. For two consecutive checkpoints take the within-pair mean of the two changes. The sample standard deviation of the 32 paired changes divided by 32 gives the reported Monte Carlo standard error of the gain. Fixed perturbations produce different realized weights as m and s change.

Backtracking tests a sampled weight objective with fixed scales. The full monitored ELBO can decrease after a cycle, and a small one-cycle change cannot establish that the parameters have stabilized. At the default budget, record cycles 0 through 60. Return the final factors and separately reevaluate the endpoint with 256 fresh antithetic draws. The preset budget gives the three priors the same number of weight-update attempts.

GLT-BNN · Step 1 of 15

Set up the regression network

Use the training rows to define the scales of the inputs and response. Carry those same scales forward to prediction.

Let K0=p and let Kl be the width of hidden layer l. Its Ml=Kl(Kl−1+1) coefficients include a bias for every receiving unit. Within a layer write a=(k,j) for one of these coefficients. The output vector v includes its intercept and has length do=KL+1.

h0=x,hl=max{0,Wl(hl−1⊤,1)⊤}
fW(x)=v⊤(hL⊤,1)⊤,Yi∣W,σ2~N(fW(xi),σ2)

Fit on predictors and responses standardized by their training sample means and standard deviations. The paper uses a Jeffreys prior p(σ2)∝1/σ2. The hidden-weight prior conditional on the scales is wa~N(0,σ2λa2). The output prior is v∣u~N(0,uI) with u=κ~C+(0,5).

Variational factors and inverse-gamma convention

Each scalar hidden or output weight has its own N(mj,sj2) factor. The noise variance, output-scale variables and variables in the selected shrinkage hierarchy have separate factors. Independence under this fitted approximation lets their moments multiply in a coordinate update.

IG(a,b):q(z)=baΓ(a)z−a−1exp(−b/z),z>0

For q(X)=IG(a,b), write ϕX=Eq[X−1]=a/b and ζX=Eq[logX]=logb−ψ(a). We abbreviate the noise precision ϕσ2 to ϕ. The same inverse-gamma shape and scale convention is used throughout.

GLT-BNN · Step 2 of 15

Initialize a single fit

Train one unpenalized network and use its fitted coefficients to initialize the variational means.

Before the warm start, sample every coefficient in a weight matrix, including biases and readout coefficients, independently from N(0,2/d), with d the number of incoming units plus one for the bias in that matrix. The standard warm start takes 600 full-batch Adam steps on squared-error loss at a fixed rate of 0.003. Set each variational standard deviation to 0.01, within the supplied log-standard-deviation bounds. The variational optimizer starts with fresh first and second moment accumulators. The lr control applies to this variational optimizer.

q(σ2)=IG(3,1),q(u)=IG(2,2),q(ρκ)=IG(1,1.04)

The starting value of ϕ is therefore 3. The output-scale auxiliary ρκ comes from u∣ρκ~IG(1/2,1/ρκ) and ρκ~IG(1/2,5−2). The next step initializes the hidden scales for the selected prior.

GLT-BNN · Step 5 of 15

Backpropagate through sampled networks

Each proposed update changes the variational means and log standard deviations of all hidden and output coefficients together.

Write ℓj=logsj. For one attempt, generate two independent standard-normal vectors and their negatives, giving four perturbations ϵ(b). Draw a complete set of weights W(b)=m+s⊙ϵ(b) for each forward pass. The sampled weight objective, up to terms fixed in this block, is

J^=−ϕ2B∑b=1B∑i=1n{yi−fW(b)(xi)}2−12∑jπj(mj2+sj2)+∑jℓj.

At draw b, begin at the output with the residual score ϕ(yi−fW(b)(xi)). Backpropagate this score through the sampled weights and the active ReLU gates. Denote the resulting derivative of the sampled data log likelihood with respect to weight wj(b) by gj(b).

∂J^∂mj=1B∑bgj(b)−πjmj,∂J^∂ℓj=1B∑bgj(b)sjϵj(b)+1−πjsj2.

The factors 1 and sjϵj(b) are the derivatives of wj(b) with respect to mj and ℓj. The terms outside the averages are the exact Gaussian prior and entropy gradients. ReLU activations and their composition across layers generally prevent a closed-form Gaussian weight update.

Trace one hidden connection

For a unit with preactivation z and activation h=max(0,z), multiply the derivative arriving from the next layer by 1{z>0}. Multiply that result by the sending activation to obtain the derivative for its incoming weight. Use a sending activation of one for a bias. Sum over training rows. The implementation uses derivative zero at z=0.

As an illustration, let f=vmax(0,wx) on one row with x=2, y=3, w=0.5, v=1 and ϕ=1. The forward pass gives f=1. The output score is 3−1=2, the gate is open, and the derivative for w is 2×1×1×2=4. If s=0.1 and ϵ=1, this draw contributes 4 to the likelihood gradient for the mean and 0.4 to that for the log standard deviation.

GLT-BNN · Step 6 of 15

Take ten successive weight-update attempts

Adam converts the weight gradients into a proposed change. Each attempt starts from the accepted or retained state of the preceding attempt.

Divide the gradient of J^ by n, clip its Euclidean norm at 10, and form the bias-corrected Adam direction. The defaults use moments with decay 0.9 and 0.999, a denominator stabilizer of 10−8 and an initial step size of 0.003. If the direction has nonpositive inner product with the gradient, use the gradient divided by the Adam denominator.

θtrial=θ+0.0032−bd,b=0,…,10,θ=(m,logs)

Clip trial log standard deviations to [−15,3]. Score the current and trial parameters with the same four perturbations and fixed prior moments. Accept the first finite candidate whose sampled objective meets or exceeds the current value. If all 11 step sizes fail, keep the current weight factors. Adam’s gradient moments and the attempt counter advance on every attempt, including rejections, and carry over between cycles. Draw fresh perturbations for the next attempt.

After ten attempts, refresh the weight moments and update the variance and shrinkage factors. Ten controls how often those factors are refreshed. A single attempt per cycle is also possible. The study used ten for all three priors.

Update the observation variance

Use the new weight moments, the current hidden-scale precisions, and a fresh estimate of the residual sum of squares.

Ha=ma2+sa2,DG=∑lMl,R≈116∑b=116∑i(yi−fW(b)(xi))2

q(σ2)=IG(n+DG2,R+∑l∑a∈IlPaHa2).

The conditional normal priors on hidden coefficients supply the DG contribution to the shape and the shrinkage-weighted sum in the scale. The output coefficients have their own prior scale. Use the new ϕ=Eq[1/σ2] in the hidden-hierarchy updates that follow.

GLT-BNN · Step 9 of 15

Update every coefficient scale

The latest expected squared weight and observation precision determine the positive scale density for each connection, including its bias.

Write Ha=ma2+sa2, ϕ=Eq(σ−2), and ϕX=Eq(X−1). Use the current factors of τl and νa, together with the refreshed Ha and ϕ from this cycle.

Aa=ϕτlϕνa,Ba=cl−2ϕHa,
q*(λa)∝λa−1exp(−Aaλa−Ba2λa2),λa>0.

Numerically evaluate ua=Eq(λa) and obtain Pa=Aaua/Ba by integration by parts. The next weight block uses cl−2ϕPa as its expected hidden-weight precision. Both ua and Pa are expectations under the fitted density.

Normalizing the density and computing moments
Za,h=∫Rexp(ht−Aaet−12Bae−2t)dt,Eq(λah)=Za,h/Za,0.

With t=logλa, the Jacobian cancels the factor λa−1. The log-scale density has mode (logBa−logAa)/3. The implementation centers at this mode and uses 32-point Gauss–Legendre panels on both sides. It also computes Eq(logλa) and the entropy for the full objective.

H{q(λa)}=logZa,0+Eq(logλa)+Aaua+12BaPa.

GLT-BNN · Step 12 of 15

Normalize the layer tail-shape factor

Use the updated global scale and all local auxiliaries to fit one tail shape for the layer.

Cl=Mlζτl+∑a∈Il(ζνa+ϕνa).

On the log scale η=logξl, the Jacobian absorbs the original 1/ξl factor. Write φ for the standard-normal density and set

gl(η)=−(Mlη+Cl)e−η−MllogΓ(e−η)−logΓ(1+Mle−η).
q*(η)=φ(η)exp{gl(η)}Zl1{η>−log2}.

The two gamma normalizers depend on the tail shape and remain in this kernel. Use adaptive quadrature over (−log2,∞) to compute its normalizing constant Zl and Rl=Eq(e−η). The bound ξl>1/2 gives 0<Rl<2. Carry this new moment into the next layer-scale and auxiliary updates.

Computing and monitoring the normalized factor
Zl=∫−log2∞φ(t)egl(t)dt,Rl=∫−log2∞e−tφ(t)egl(t)dtZl.

The implementation stabilizes the integrals on the log scale. Its default relative tolerance is 10−10 with up to 2,000 subdivisions per segment. A roundoff-limited segment can be retried at 10−8. It also computes the mean of η, its entropy, and the expected log-density contributions to the ELBO. The truncation probability of the Gaussian prior is constant in the coordinate update and is restored in the full objective.

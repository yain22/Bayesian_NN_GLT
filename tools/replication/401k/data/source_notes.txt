Benchmark source

The analysis uses all 9,915 complete households in the public sipp1991.dta
file from the authors' double machine learning replication repository. This is
also the source used by the DoubleML R package's fetch_401k(). The preparation
archive includes the original data file and the analysis CSV with the five
fold assignments. The preparation script rebuilds the CSV from the bundled
Stata file and can retrieve that file from the fixed revision if needed.

Chernozhukov and Hansen (2004) describe this sample as the fourth wave of
the 1990 Survey of Income and Program Participation panel, collected in 1991.
Reference persons were aged 25 to 64, with at least one employed person and
no self-employed person in the household. Net total financial assets are
measured in 1991 US dollars. Eligibility is an offer of an employer-sponsored
401(k) plan. The treatment is household eligibility, not actual enrollment.
The nine adjustment variables follow the original benchmark. They are the
household head's age and education, income, household size, married status,
two-earner status, defined-benefit pension status, IRA participation and
home ownership. No covariate is derived from the response or 401(k)
participation variable. Whether the covariates suffice for exchangeability
is an identification assumption, not a consequence of the data.

There are 3,682 eligible and 6,233 ineligible households. Unadjusted group
means are 30,347.39 and 10,788.04 dollars, giving a difference of 19,559.34
dollars computed before rounding. The preparation script creates one
treatment-stratified random partition with 1,983 observations per fold.
Folds 1 through 5 contain 736, 736, 736, 737 and 737 eligible households.
Within treatment group, indices in original source order are shuffled with
R's Mersenne-Twister (Rejection sampler, seed 401015), then allocated in
round-robin order, starting the second stratum where the first leaves off.

The published DML analysis in Chernozhukov et al. (2018, Section 6.2,
Table 2, Panel A) gives five-fold interactive-model ATE estimates ranging
from 7,170 to 8,105 dollars across seven learners or combinations. For their
neural network, the estimate is 7,788 dollars, with median within-split SE
1,238 and split-adjusted SE 1,293. Their point estimate is the median across
100 random partitions. Their implementation excludes observations whose
propensity scores fall outside the open interval (0.01, 0.99). A full-sample
analysis which instead bounds the fitted probabilities uses different
regularization.

The U.S. Census Bureau lists the 1990 panel, wave 4, as public-use microdata.
Chapter 5 of the SIPP Users' Guide permits copying and distribution of
released SIPP data. The Stata file is the research sample distributed by
the original double machine learning authors. The CSV contains its analysis
variables, a sequential row number and the study's fold assignments.
